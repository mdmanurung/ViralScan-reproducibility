## Recovery of the expected viruses

HSV-1 has primary sequence calls in all 14 exposed libraries and none of the
five unexposed libraries. Only 11/14 exposed libraries are target-resolved
under the frozen sibling rule. GSM3511322, GSM3511323 and GSM3511330 have
zero, zero and one HSV-1-specific molecules, respectively, and remain
clade-only. They must not be included in an HSV-1-resolved sensitivity claim.
The retained per-cell table shows increasing high-burden cell fractions over
time within each exposure protocol; a library exposure label does not imply
that every cell is infected.

HPV16 is supported in the positive raft library, with 12,995 counted
virus-best molecules in 2,913 cells in the tier table, and no primary call in
the parental library. Matrix-based cell counts in other tables use a
different assignment definition and must be labelled separately. The parental
library's 16 non-called HPV16 barcodes, 12 shared with the positive library,
are consistent with swapping. The source study analysed 4,275 positive-raft
and 3,833 negative-raft cells; the substantially larger frozen ViralScan
called-barcode sets are not comparable denominators. See
[Bedard et al., final paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-37377-0).

EBV has supported primary sequence evidence in all three LCLs. The type-2
label belongs to the same sibling group and does not establish an independent
infection. The final source paper reports lytic-cluster frequencies of 2.2%
and 0.9% in B95-8 and M81; those clusters include cells with about 3–15%
lytic transcripts. They are not defined simply by a >10% cutoff. ViralScan's
8.8% and 1.2% fractions above 10% total EBV transcripts are therefore a
different measurement and cannot be described as reproducing the paper's
lytic-cell fractions. See
[SoRelle et al., final paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7867410/).

HNSCC has supported HPV16 in all three downloaded HPV-positive tumour CD45−
libraries. The 27 HPV-negative libraries have no primary HPV16 call. Among
the remaining HPV-positive libraries, a CD45+ library has only one molecule
and another has no call; two PBL libraries have no call. These are distinct
compartments, not equivalent positive controls. The full source cohort had
six HPV-positive and twelve HPV-negative patients, while the downloaded
subset covers four HPV-positive patients. See
[Kürten et al., final paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-021-27619-4).

HHV-6B high-burden cell fractions are concordant in scale with the source
study but do not use identical cells. The retained D98 estimate is 0.305%
of called cells; the final paper reports 0.2% carrying 99.2% of viral UMIs.
The paper also reports 49% and 62% high-expressing T cells after extended
culture, so high late-timepoint fractions are not intrinsically discordant.
Donor 38 was negative by the paper's cell-expression and U31 qPCR assays;
GSM6416026 has no ≥10-molecule HHV-6B cells here. It is a useful empirical
control, not proof that every possible viral exposure is absent. Six
VDJ-enriched TCR libraries are retained for detection accounting but excluded
from host-program fitting. See
[Lareau et al., final paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10999258/).

The final HSV-1 paper confirms the >2,000-gene cell filter, the 1/3/5-hour
timepoints and the high-cell 8–30% viral-mRNA range. ViralScan's frozen cell
calling and molecular assignments differ, so the recovery comparison is
descriptive. See
[Wyler et al., final paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-12894-z).

must remain a cross-mapping/clade interpretation. HHV-6A-labelled reads can
include provisional label-specific molecules, but weighted counts and small
alignment-score margins do not establish an independent HHV-6A infection.
Strain divergence and cross-mapping remain alternative explanations.

The six locus-restricted HIV-1 libraries have almost no depth in reference
positions 700–7,100, supporting a vector-like rather than genome-wide

"HIV-1-derived sequence consistent with vector material" is the bounded
interpretation. Vaccinia barcode sharing across HNSCC libraries supports
carry-over or a shared barcode artefact; it does not identify the physical
contamination mechanism.
